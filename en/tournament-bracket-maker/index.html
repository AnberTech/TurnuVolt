How to make a tournament bracket

A bracket shows who plays whom and where each winner goes next. Here is how to work out byes, place your seeds and run a fair draw for a knockout tournament.

How does a single-elimination bracket work?

In a single-elimination (knockout) tournament the loser is out and the winner moves on to the next round. After any first-round byes, the field halves each round, and whoever wins the final is the champion. Because exactly one team is eliminated in every game, a bracket with n teams always has n − 1 games: 16 teams need 4 rounds and 15 games to crown a champion. The format is quick, but one bad day can knock out even a strong team. If you want everyone to play more games, consider a round-robin league, or group play followed by a knockout stage.

When the team count isn't a power of two: byes

For a bracket to work, the number of teams left after the first round must be a power of two (2, 4, 8, 16, 32…). If your team count doesn't fit, some teams get a bye: they skip the first round and go straight to the second. The number of byes is the next power of two minus the number of teams. With 6 teams, 8 − 6 = 2 teams get a bye; with 10 teams, 16 − 10 = 6; with 12 teams, 16 − 12 = 4. The other teams play in the first round, and from round two on the bracket has an exact power of two.

Seeding and random draws

Seeding means placing the strongest teams far apart in the bracket so they can't meet in the early rounds. In a classic 8-team bracket, the No. 1 seed plays No. 8, No. 2 plays No. 7, and seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final. If there are byes, giving them to the top seeds is a common approach. If you don't use seeds, set the matchups with a random draw: drawing names in front of everyone, or using a tool that shuffles the order, can make the bracket feel fairer. Whichever method you choose, announce it before the tournament starts.

Round names and planning

In a full bracket, rounds are named after the number of teams left: 16 teams is the Round of 16, then the quarterfinals (8), the semifinals (4) and the final (2). In a bracket with byes, the first round may be named after the bracket's power-of-two size, so with 6 teams the first round counts as the quarterfinals. Count the rounds too: 8 teams need 3, 16 teams need 4 and 32 teams need 5. Give every game a number and write the next round as “Winner of game 3” and so on, so the bracket makes sense before results come in. Some tournaments add a third-place game.

Frequently asked questions

What is a bye in a tournament bracket?

A bye means a team advances to the next round without playing. Byes balance the bracket when the number of teams isn't a power of two. They often go to the top seeds or to teams picked in a draw; the details depend on your tournament's rules.

How many byes do I need?

Find the next power of two that is equal to or greater than your team count, then subtract the number of teams. 5 teams need 3 byes, 6 teams need 2, 7 teams need 1 and 9 teams need 7. That leaves an even 4, 8 or 16 teams in round two.

How many games are in a single-elimination tournament?

A single-elimination tournament with n teams always has n − 1 games, because one team is knocked out in each game and everyone except the champion loses exactly once. 8 teams play 7 games, 12 teams play 11 and 32 teams play 31. A third-place game adds one more.

How do you decide the seeds?

Seeds are usually based on past results, the standings from a league or group stage, or the organizer's judgment. Which method you use depends on your tournament's rules. Explaining the criteria in advance can reduce arguments about the seeding later.
